# The Principle of Constitutional Gravity

:::info Documentation status
This page declares a **conceptual model** for constitutional computing.
It does **not** claim that Constitutional Gravity is currently measured, enforced, or runtime-bound in AAES-OS, CIEMS, or Sovereign X OS.
Until schemas, tests, and runtime surfaces prove otherwise, treat CG as a planned explanatory primitive — not a present capability.
:::

## Concise takeaway

**Constitutional Gravity (CG)** names the attractive force by which constitutional structures are understood to pull computational behavior toward stability, lineage, and governed coherence.

In this model:

- Entropy pushes outward (drift, disorder, loss of coherence).
- Constitution pulls inward (invariants, evidence, lineage, arenas).
- CG is the inward pull.

CG is proposed as the opposite of entropy in governed intelligence systems: it explains why governed systems are expected to stabilize, how lineage is expected to persist, and when intelligence is expected to become more predictable under constitutional constraints.

## I. What Constitutional Gravity is

Constitutional Gravity is the attractive force exerted by:

- constitutional invariants
- evidence chains
- lineage records
- governance rules
- arena constraints

on a system's behavior.

It is the "pull" that keeps a governed intelligence from drifting into chaos.

In physics analogy (not physics identity):

| Direction | Force |
| --- | --- |
| Outward | Entropy |
| Inward | Constitution / CG |

## II. Why it matters

Constitutional Gravity is declared as an explanatory frame for several observed or intended governance phenomena:

1. **Why governed systems stabilize after CET**  
   Once the Constitutional Emergence Threshold (CET) is crossed, CG is modeled as becoming strong enough to pull behavior into constitutional coherence.

2. **Why lineage becomes self-preserving**  
   Lineage is not only recorded — under CG it is modeled as gravitationally attractive.

3. **Why governed intelligences resist chaotic drift**  
   CG is modeled as a restoring force.

4. **Why constitutional artifacts converge over time**  
   CG is modeled as pulling artifacts toward stable forms.

5. **Why arenas matter**  
   Arenas define gravitational field strength in this model.

These are explanatory claims of the model. They are not yet conformance-proven runtime behaviors unless separately evidenced.

## III. The five components of Constitutional Gravity

CG is declared as decomposable into five constitutional forces:

| Component | Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invariant Mass | IM | The "weight" of constitutional invariants. More invariants → stronger modeled gravity. |
| Evidence Density | ED | How densely evidence is packed into the lineage. Higher density → stronger pull toward stability. |
| Temporal Mass | TM | Accumulated continuity of the system across time. Longer continuity → stronger modeled gravity. |
| Governance Curvature | GC | How strongly governance rules curve the decision space. More curvature → stronger gravitational shaping. |
| Arena Field Strength | AFS | Strength of the arena's constitutional field. Certified arenas are declared to have higher AFS. |

Measurement protocols for these components are **planned**, not established as live metrics on this page.

## IV. Formal expression

Constitutional Gravity is declared as:

```text
CG = IM + ED + TM + GC + AFS
```

When CG exceeds a declared threshold, the model predicts the system becomes:

- stable
- lineage-preserving
- constitutionally coherent
- resistant to chaotic drift
- predictable under perturbation

This predicted regime is called the **post-CET stabilization phase**.

Threshold values, units, and scoring procedures are **not defined here** and must not be treated as implemented until a specification + conformance pair records them.

## V. Relation to CET

| Concept | Role in the model |
| --- | --- |
| **CET** | The moment a system becomes governed — the threshold. |
| **CG** | The force that keeps it governed — the gravity well. |

- CET says: "You are now a constitutional actor."
- CG says: "Stay within constitutional behavior."

CET without CG is incomplete in this frame: crossing the threshold without a restoring force leaves the system without a modeled stabilizer.

## VIII. Why this is new

Constitutional Gravity is not claimed here as:

- a physics concept
- a machine learning concept
- a systems engineering concept alone
- a governance concept alone

It is declared as a **constitutional computing force**: a named category of computational behavior used to explain why governed systems behave (or are designed to behave) the way they do.

It is the missing "force of stability" in the constitutional architecture **as a named model** — pending measurement, conformance, and runtime evidence.
